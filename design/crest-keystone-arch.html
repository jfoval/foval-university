How the two became one

The arch supplies the institution and the openness: a gate with nothing in it, and a path with its steps drawn in climbing through. The keystone supplies the identity: it is the one stone that holds everything else up, it sits dead centre bearing both sides equally, and it carries the F cut in like a mason's mark. Rather than sitting inside the arch as decoration, the keystone is oversized and projects above and below the ring of stones, which is how real keystones are cut. It is the thing your eye lands on first.

The masonry joints are the detail that makes it one object instead of two symbols stacked. They turn the arch into built stone, so the keystone has something to hold. At small sizes the joints and the F disappear on their own and you are left with the simplest version: arch, gold stone, open ground. That is the app icon.

Colour logic: navy is the stone, gold is the one stone that matters, white is the open air through the gate. Three colours, all flat, so it prints in two inks (gold can drop to navy) and embroiders cleanly.
