# What Foval Learning Institute Stands On

**Faith. Knowledge. Life. Free for everyone.**

These are the values every decision here runs against: what we teach, how we write it, which feedback we act on, and which we set aside. They are short on purpose. If a proposal fits them, it's probably right. If it doesn't, no amount of enthusiasm makes it right.

## 1. Free, for everyone

No tuition, no ads, no paywalls, no account needed to learn. The cost of a real education should be zero, and the door should have no lock. We'd rather grow slowly than fund this with anything that compromises the learner.

## 2. Truth, and honesty about it

We say what is known, how it's known, and what isn't settled. Every checkable claim has a source. Every lesson is fact-checked against the primary literature in a separate pass. Where experts genuinely disagree, we teach the disagreement at full strength on every side. Where the evidence has settled something, we say so plainly and don't pretend otherwise to keep everyone comfortable. We would rather write "we don't know" than write something confident and wrong.

## 3. Faith, taught honestly

We believe Jesus is Lord and that the Bible is true, and the School of Christian Studies teaches from within that faith and says so on every course. That is not an exception to honesty; it's an application of it. A learner always knows which kind of course they're in. The rest of the institute teaches on neutral ground and never mocks belief or unbelief. Nothing here sets out to undermine faith, and nothing here hides evidence. Those are the same commitment.

## 4. Depth over polish

Real content, written from the standard references, with worked examples, mechanisms, and the mistakes experts know beginners make. Nothing that merely sounds authoritative. If a lesson could have been written by someone who didn't know the subject, it isn't a lesson yet.

## 5. The whole person

Books and street smarts. History, philosophy, mathematics, science, and literature alongside money, sales, health, survival, and how to use AI. A Foval graduate should be well-read and hard to fool, able to hold a conversation about Aristotle and fix a flat tyre. Universities stopped teaching half of this. We won't.

## 6. Plain speech

A good professor talking to one student across a table. Short words, examples first, the mistakes named, the limits admitted. No lecturing, no selling, no machine-sounding prose. If you'd be embarrassed to say a sentence to a real person, it doesn't ship.

## 7. Learning that lasts

Reading is the weakest form of learning there is, so a lesson here makes you think while you read: predict before you're told, check yourself as you go, do the work before the quiz, and come back to it on a schedule so it stays learned. A transcript here means you can still do the thing, not that you once saw a page about it.

## 8. Built with the people learning from it

Every lesson asks for feedback, and every piece of feedback is read. What makes a lesson clearer, deeper, or more honest gets built in. What would make it shallower, slanted, or softer on the truth gets set aside, with a reason. The learners improve the institute; the values decide how.

## 9. Open

The whole institute, content and code, lives in the open. Anyone can read how a lesson was researched, reviewed, and revised. Anyone can propose a course. The content is licensed for reuse. An education that costs nothing should also hide nothing.

---

*How these are used:* the editorial standards (`docs/EDITORIAL_STANDARDS.md`) and style guide (`docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md`) are these values made specific. The feedback loop (`docs/FEEDBACK_LOOP.md`) filters learner feedback against them. When two values seem to conflict, honesty about what's true comes first, and being free for everyone comes second.
