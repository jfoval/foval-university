## 2026-09-04 — Lesson 02 — depth, fact-check, neutrality, pedagogy
**Reviewer:** depth / fact-check / neutrality / pedagogy (four fresh-context subagents; fact-checker verified against primary texts and the web)

**Findings (ranked):**

*High*
1. [high, depth + fact] "she knows immediately that it is the version that favours the indemnifying party" — the law is backwards. A gross-negligence/wilful-misconduct carve-out bites less often than an ordinary-negligence one, so the indemnified party keeps its cover more often; practitioner sources confirm the indemnitee prefers the gross-negligence form. Fix: favours the *indemnified* party; the other side would push for "negligence".
2. [high, fact] "showed players a position from a real game for a few seconds ... Masters rebuilt far more of the board than beginners" — Chase & Simon 1973 tested three individuals (a master, a Class A player, a beginner), 5-second exposures, trials to criterion; trial-1 recall 81/49/33%. Game-position superiority was de Groot's finding; the random-position result in detail is Chase & Simon's ("no relation at all" with skill). "Largely vanished" matches the later literature (Gobet & Simon 1996b: ~1 piece per 400 Elo residual), not the 1973 paper. Fix: state the design, credit de Groot, cite the residual.
3. [high, fact] "Herbert Simon later estimated a master's stock of chunks at roughly 50,000, a figure Gobet and Simon revisited in 1998" — the estimate is Simon & Gilmartin 1973 (range 10,000–100,000; 50,000 the midpoint that stuck), same year not "later"; Gobet & Simon 1996 and 1998 *upheld* it; later CHREST models used ~300,000. Fix: rewrite history; add Simon & Gilmartin 1973.
4. [high, depth + pedagogy] Quiz: Q2, Q4 and Q5 answerable by recognising the lesson's own phrasing (Q4 nearly verbatim from the notes paragraph); correct answer is the longest option in Q1–Q4, extreme in Q2 (38 words vs 13–17). Fix: replace Q4 with an activity-vs-thought case; rebalance Q2 distractor lengths; add a Q6 testing objective 2 (chunk-count diagnostic).
5. [high, neutrality] "First, *factual knowledge precedes skill*. You cannot "think critically" about ..." and Go deeper "why facts precede skill" — the cognitive claim is well supported but "precedes" is a sequencing claim in the live knowledge-rich vs inquiry debate (SOURCES.md contested item 6), resolved in the author's voice; scare quotes on "think critically" sneer; "beginners resist" pre-frames disagreement as naivety. Fix: state the cognitive claim, flag the sequencing question as open, drop scare quotes.
6. [high, pedagogy] Objective 3 ("identify extraneous load ... and rewrite") has no worked example before exercise step 4 asks for it, and the learner has no test to tell extraneous from intrinsic load. Fix: add a before/after rewrite with the test "if the rewrite changes no content and the passage gets easier, the load was extraneous".

*Medium*
7. [medium, depth] "Memory is the residue of thought" section has no evidence; "roughly in proportion to how you thought about it" hedges; "in the form you want it back" is unattributed. Fix: add Craik & Tulving 1975 (levels of processing) and Morris, Bransford & Franks 1977 (transfer-appropriate processing).
8. [medium, depth + fact] "Decorative animation, background music, a lecturer's tangent ... Sweller's ... split-attention and redundancy effects are the evidence" and Q3 explain — music/decoration is Mayer's coherence effect (Moreno & Mayer 2000), not Sweller's redundancy effect (which is presenting the same information twice). Split-attention asserted without a named experiment. Fix: attribute correctly; name Chandler & Sweller 1991.
9. [medium, depth] "the note-taking literature is messier than the slogans suggest" — hedging filler when SOURCES.md has Chi et al. 1989 (self-explainers vs rereaders/copiers) and the Mueller & Oppenheimer non-replications (Morehead et al. 2019; Urry et al. 2021). [medium, fact + neutrality + depth] "a matter of a slot or so, and you cannot train the raw number very far ... the overwhelmingly likely reason is their chunks" — uncited; understates that WM capacity is one of the stronger correlates of reasoning (Shipstead, Harrison & Engle 2016). "Cannot train" is supported (Melby-Lervåg & Hulme 2013; Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme 2016; Simons et al. 2016). Fix: cite; give Cowan's 3–5 range; acknowledge the correlation; drop "overwhelmingly".

Also missing the CLT point that the limit applies to *novel* material. Fix: one-sentence caveat, "argued", "for almost everyone", add the novel-material point.
15. [medium, neutrality + fact] Oakley callout: "uses "chunking" for the whole process of practising a skill until it runs smoothly.
