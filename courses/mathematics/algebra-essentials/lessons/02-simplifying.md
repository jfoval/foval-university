Expressions can often be written more compactly without changing their value. That's simplifying.

## Collecting like terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same variable part. `3x` and `5x` are like terms. `3x` and `3x²` are not. `3x` and `3y` are not.

`3x + 5x = 8x` (three x's plus five x's is eight x's)

`4a + 2b − a + 3b = 3a + 5b`

`x² + 2x + 3x² − x = 4x² + x`

## Expanding brackets

A number outside a bracket multiplies *everything* inside. This is the distributive law.

`3(x + 4) = 3x + 12`

`−2(a − 5) = −2a + 10` (watch the signs: negative times negative is positive)

`x(x + 3) = x² + 3x`

## Two brackets

Multiply every term in the first by every term in the second. Four products for two two-term brackets.

`(x + 2)(x + 5) = x² + 5x + 2x + 10 = x² + 7x + 10`

`(x − 3)(x + 3) = x² + 3x − 3x − 9 = x² − 9`

That last one is worth remembering: **(a − b)(a + b) = a² − b²**, the "difference of two squares".

## Factorising: expanding in reverse

Find what's common to every term and pull it outside.

`6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)`

`x² + 4x = x(x + 4)`

Check by expanding back. If you get what you started with, it's right.

:::callout Common slip
`(x + 3)² is NOT x² + 9`. Write it as `(x + 3)(x + 3) = x² + 6x + 9`. The middle term never disappears.
:::
