A linear equation in two variables, like `y = 2x + 1`, describes a straight line. Every point (x, y) on the line satisfies the equation. Graphs turn algebra into pictures.

## Slope: the rate of change

**Slope** (also called gradient) is how much y changes for each 1 unit increase in x. In `y = 2x + 1`, the slope is 2: every step right goes 2 up.

From two points: slope = (change in y) ÷ (change in x) = "rise over run".

Between (1, 3) and (4, 12): slope = (12 − 3)/(4 − 1) = 9/3 = 3.

-   Positive slope: line rises left to right.
-   Negative slope: line falls.
-   Zero slope: horizontal line.
-   Bigger absolute value: steeper.

## Intercept: where it starts

The **y-intercept** is the y value when x = 0, where the line crosses the vertical axis. In `y = 2x + 1`, it's 1.

## Slope-intercept form

**y = mx + c**, where m is the slope and c is the y-intercept. Any straight line can be written this way (except vertical ones). Given the form, you can sketch the line instantly: start at c, then go up m for every 1 across.

## Finding the equation from data

A plumber charges a call-out fee plus an hourly rate. A 2-hour job costs $110; a 5-hour job costs $230. Find the formula.

Slope = (230 − 110)/(5 − 2) = 120/3 = 40 per hour.

Use one point: 110 = 40(2) + c, so c = 30.

Formula: `cost = 40h + 30`. Call-out fee $30, $40 per hour.

:::callout Why this matters
Slope is the algebra of "rate". Speed, price per unit, interest rate, growth per year: all slopes. When you can read a slope, you can read the world's straight-line relationships.
:::
