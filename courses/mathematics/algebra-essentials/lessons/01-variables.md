A **variable** is a letter that stands in for a number we don't know yet, or a number that can change. That's it. Everything else in algebra follows from taking that idea seriously.

## Expressions

An **expression** combines numbers, variables, and operations: `3x + 5`, `2a − b`, `x²`. It doesn't claim anything; it's just a recipe for a number once you know what the variables are.

Conventions to know:

-   `3x` means 3 × x. Multiplication signs are dropped between a number and a letter.
-   `x²` means x × x.
-   `x/4` means x ÷ 4.
-   A plain `x` is `1x`. The 1 is invisible.

## Evaluating

To evaluate an expression, substitute the value and do the arithmetic.

If x = 4: `3x + 5 = 3(4) + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17`

If a = 2 and b = 7: `2a − b = 4 − 7 = −3`

## Order of operations

Brackets, then powers, then multiplication and division (left to right), then addition and subtraction (left to right). Many wrong answers come from skipping this.

`2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14`, not 20.

## From words to symbols

| Words | Algebra |
| --- | --- |
| five more than a number | n + 5 |
| twice a number | 2n |
| a number decreased by 3 | n − 3 |
| half a number | n/2 |
| the sum of two numbers | a + b |
| three less than twice a number | 2n − 3 |
