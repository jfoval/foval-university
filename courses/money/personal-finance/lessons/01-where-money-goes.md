You cannot manage what you do not measure. The single highest-return activity in personal finance is spending one hour finding out exactly where last month's money went.

## The exercise

1.  Open your bank and card statements for the last full month.
2.  Put every transaction into one of these buckets:
    -   **Housing** (rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance)
    -   **Food** (groceries separate from eating out)
    -   **Transport**
    -   **Debt payments**
    -   **Subscriptions and recurring**
    -   **Everything else**
3.  Total each bucket and compare it to your take-home pay.

Most people are surprised twice: once by eating out, and once by subscriptions they forgot they had.

## Fixed versus variable

Split your spending into **fixed** costs (the same every month, hard to change quickly: rent, insurance, loan payments) and **variable** costs (food, fun, shopping). Fixed costs determine how much room you have. Variable costs are where day-to-day choices live.

> If your fixed costs alone exceed about 60% of take-home pay, your budget will feel tight no matter how disciplined you are. The fix is structural, not willpower.

## Needs, wants, and the 50/30/20 sketch

A popular rule of thumb: roughly 50% of take-home pay on needs, 30% on wants, 20% on saving and paying down debt. It is a sketch, not a law. High-cost cities break it. Its value is in forcing the question: is this a need or a want?

:::callout Don't judge, just look
The goal of this lesson is information, not guilt. You are gathering data so the next decisions are informed ones.
:::
