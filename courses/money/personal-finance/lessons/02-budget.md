A budget is not a punishment. It's a plan for your money that you write before the month begins, so the money goes where you actually want it to.

## Why budgets fail

-   They are too detailed (37 categories nobody will update).
-   They forget irregular costs: car repairs, gifts, annual fees, holidays.
-   They leave no room for fun, so they get abandoned the first weekend.

## The four-line budget

Start with something you can keep in your head:

| Line | What goes here |
| --- | --- |
| 1\. Fixed costs | Rent, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments, subscriptions you keep |
| 2\. Sinking funds | Money set aside monthly for irregular costs (see below) |
| 3\. Savings and extra debt payments | Emergency fund first, then goals or debt |
| 4\. Everything else | Groceries, transport, fun. One number. Spend it however you like. |

Take-home pay minus lines 1 to 3 equals line 4. That's your spending money. When it's gone, it's gone.

## Sinking funds: the secret weapon

List every irregular expense for the year and divide by 12. A $600 car insurance bill every six months is $100 a month. A $1,200 holiday is $100 a month. Put that money into a separate account each month. When the bill arrives, the money is already there and it never feels like a crisis.

## Pay yourself first

Set up an automatic transfer to savings on payday, before you can spend it. People who wait to save "whatever is left" almost always have nothing left. Automation beats discipline.

## The emergency fund

Before investing, before paying extra on low-rate debt, build a cushion:

1.  **Starter:** $1,000 (or one month of line 1) as fast as possible.
2.  **Full:** 3 to 6 months of essential expenses in a separate savings account.

The fund is not for holidays or sales. It's for job loss, medical bills, and the boiler breaking. Its real product is not money; it's sleep.
